Most recognition
does not survive the week.

It is not that people do not care. It is that appreciation has nowhere to live. A message scrolls away. A shout-out ends when the meeting does.

  • A message in the team channelgone in 40 minutes
  • A line at the end of standupheard by nine people
  • An email nobody replies toarchived unread
  • The annual revieweleven months late
  • A gift card with no notespent, forgotten
  • Only from managersthe rest stays unsaid

Halo does one thing instead: it makes appreciation a small, beautiful object that stays. On a profile. In a career. Long after the project ends.

See what a card is →

More than a message.
A memory.

Every card is a designed object with a sender, a moment and a date. It lands in someone's profile and it stays there for as long as they work with you.

  • The moment

    Great work, thank you, a birthday, an anniversary, a promotion, a welcome, a farewell.

  • The words

    Written by a person. Optionally shaped by AI, never sent by it.

  • The signatures

    Colleagues can add their name to a card before it is sent.

  • The record

    Private by default. The sender decides who else sees it.

Deliberately small.

Halo is not an HR suite. There is no payroll, no performance review cycle, no survey tool. Six things, each in service of one.
