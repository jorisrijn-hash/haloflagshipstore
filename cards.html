Appreciation cards

The whole product is
a single object.

Everything in Halo exists to make one thing worth receiving: a card that says what someone actually did, signed by the people who saw it.

Write one now Browse templates →

Six decisions,
then it is gone.

A card is deliberately shallow. Every field earns its place, and none of them is a formality you can leave blank and still have it mean something.

  • Who

    One recipient. Cards are not broadcast to a group; a group can sign one.

  • The moment

    Great work, thank you, birthday, anniversary, promotion, welcome, farewell.

  • The words

    280 characters. Short enough to be specific, too short to be generic.

  • The design

    A template, not a colour picker. Someone else already made it look right.

  • The signatures

    Colleagues add their name before it sends. One card, many voices.

  • Who can see it

    Private, team, or company. Private is the default, always.

They accumulate.

A card does not expire when the project ends or the manager leaves. Scroll, and you are looking at what a recognition history actually is: a stack of specific moments that belongs to the person who earned it, not to the company.

When someone leaves, the workspace keeps the record and they keep a personal export of every card they received.

Private until
somebody chooses otherwise.

Praise that is performed for an audience is worth less than praise that is meant. So the default is the quiet one.

Private by default

A new card is visible to the sender, the recipient and anyone who signed it. Nothing else, and no feed entry anywhere.

Team visible

Shared into one department, where the work actually happened.

Company visible

For milestones the whole organisation should see.

Workspace policy

An admin can set a default and cap the ceiling, so nobody accidentally publishes something that was meant to stay between two people.
